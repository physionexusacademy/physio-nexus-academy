# 2. Lay summary (about 250 words)

**English.** Many people do exercises at home for neck and back problems, but they often cannot tell whether they are doing the exercise correctly. We have built a feature for our exercise website that uses the camera of a phone to count repetitions and to estimate the angle of the neck or back during an exercise. Before we ever let patients see numbers from it, we need to know how accurate it is. In this study, 8 to 10 healthy adult volunteers will do eight gentle neck and back movements twice, about one hour in total, with a physiotherapist present. For each movement the physiotherapist measures the angle with a standard instrument and the camera measures it too, and we compare the two. For a few movements the volunteer will make a small, safe, deliberate mistake so that we can see whether the tool notices. No video or photograph is saved or sent anywhere; only numbers next to a code are kept, never a name. The risks are very small (a little muscle tiredness) and volunteers can stop at any time. There is no direct benefit to volunteers; the benefit is that we find out honestly whether the tool is good enough to be offered to patients, and for which movements, or whether it should not be used. The results will be published as group results only.

# 4. Answers to likely committee questions (for your preparation)

**Why healthy volunteers and not patients?** Because the first question is purely technical, how closely a camera reading agrees with a standard instrument, and answering it in people without pain removes any risk of provoking symptoms. Patients enter only in a second, separate, supervised step, and only for movements that met the accuracy rules.

**Why only 8 to 10 people?** It is a pilot whose purpose is to estimate bias and variability so that a confirmatory study can be sized properly, and to decide what the tool may show in the supervised patient step. The protocol says so, reports wide intervals as wide and makes no confirmatory claim (protocol 10.6). It is also the smallest number that exposes more than one phone, one clothing type and one body size, which is a safety question for later.

**Why is nothing recorded? Would recorded video not make the research better?** Recording would let the analysis be re-run, but it is not needed for the primary question and it would create identifiable images of the body. We chose the smallest data that answer the question. If the committee prefers a recorded-video or landmark-only option, a separate optional module with its own consent and safeguards is drafted (document 15); we would not add it without your approval.

**What if the tool is wrong?** That is what is being measured, and in the study no number is used for any decision about the participant. In later use, a number is shown only for movement types that met the pre-specified rules; otherwise only counts and timers are shown, or nothing.

**Could a cue make someone go too far?** Cues are fixed sentences checked by an automatic wording test in the build: they never ask for more range, effort or force, never ride over pain and never state a medical judgement; "stop if anything hurts, feels dizzy or tingles" appears on every set-up screen and summary. Cues that tell a person to stop or ease off exist; cues that ask for more do not.

**You have a commercial interest. How is bias controlled?** Registered protocol and decision rules, frozen software (hashes recorded), blinded reference assessor, camera reading hidden from the assessor until the reference is entered, independent analyst, full reporting of all pre-specified analyses and of failures, and the interest declared in the consent form and in every publication.

**Is this a medical device?** In the study and the supervised pilot the software is used for exercise guidance and research, not for diagnosis or treatment decisions. The regulatory position for any wider release or any measurement claim will be checked under the rules that apply in [TO COMPLETE: jurisdiction] before it happens.

**What about staff and students taking part?** Allowed only if there is no reporting or assessment relationship with the investigators, with a clear statement that declining has no consequence; their records are labelled so this can be checked (protocol 5).

**What happens to the data and for how long?** Numbers only, with a study code; stored in the application's access-controlled database with an audit log; paper key held by the PI; de-identified export to the analyst; retained [TO COMPLETE] then destroyed; aggregate publication; withdrawal possible before analysis lock (data management plan, document 07).

**How do you protect a participant who is shy about being on camera?** The participant sees only themselves, nothing is recorded, a same-sex assessor or chaperone is offered, a "simple view" shows only the skeleton, and the participant may stop at any time.

**What about clothing, body size and head covering?** They are recorded as conditions, never as reasons to exclude, and never to remove clothing; the tool's performance for them is reported so that its limits are known.

**What do you do if someone is hurt?** The physiotherapist is present, stops the movement at once, asks about the symptom, records it on the adverse-event form (document 10) and reports it to the committee as it requires (document 09).
