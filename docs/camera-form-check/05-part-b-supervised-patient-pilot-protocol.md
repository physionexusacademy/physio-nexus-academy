## 1. Summary

A supervised, single-arm, prospective pilot in 8 to 10 adult patients of the clinic who have been given neck or back exercises. They use the camera feature of RehabVerse during their normal exercises, with the physiotherapist present for the first session and the physiotherapist's oversight afterwards. The study asks whether the feature can be used safely and acceptably in real patients, how often it cannot give a reading, how well its counts and cues agree with the physiotherapist's own judgement, and what changes are needed before any larger study. It does **not** test treatment effect and makes no claim of diagnosis.

## 2. Objectives

**Primary (feasibility and safety).** The proportion of patients who complete the planned camera sessions, and the number and severity of adverse events and of exercise-related pain increases attributed to the tool's use.

**Secondary.**

1. Coverage: the proportion of sets in which the tool gives a reading, and the reasons it cannot (set-up not met, tracking lost, low confidence).
2. Agreement of the tool's repetition count and hold time with the physiotherapist's own count and a stopwatch in observed sets.
3. Agreement between the tool's cue and the physiotherapist's judgement about the same set (the physiotherapist rates, blind to the tool's cue, whether a fault was present).
4. Usability and acceptability: System Usability Scale, short comfort items, and open comments from patients and from the physiotherapists.
5. Adherence: sessions completed with and without the camera over the pilot period (descriptive).
6. The time the camera set-up takes, and the support it needs.

## 3. Design

Single-arm, prospective, supervised feasibility and safety pilot, [TO COMPLETE: duration, for example 2 weeks]. No control group and no comparison of outcomes. Use of the camera is an **addition** to usual care and never replaces it; the exercises, their dose and the clinical decisions are the physiotherapist's, exactly as without the tool.

## 4. Participants

**Number.** 8 to 10 patients, justified as a feasibility and safety pilot (section 9).

**Inclusion.** Age 18 to 65 years; attending the clinic for neck, mid-back or low-back pain and given exercises by a study physiotherapist; at least one assigned exercise is one of the movement types that met the Part A rules; has a smartphone or computer with a camera and a stable internet connection, or uses a clinic device; able to give informed consent in English or Urdu.

**Exclusion.** Any red flag or condition for which the treating physiotherapist considers exercise unsuitable or in need of medical assessment first (examples to be listed by the physiotherapist: suspected fracture, infection, cancer, cauda equina features, progressive neurological loss, inflammatory spinal disease, unexplained weight loss, severe night pain); dizziness, fainting or drop attacks with neck movement (for neck exercises); recent spinal surgery [TO COMPLETE: period]; pregnancy beyond the first trimester for floor positions; inability to get to and from the floor if floor exercises are assigned; vision or cognitive limitation that makes the screen unusable; unwillingness to be on camera (no pressure is applied).

**Recruitment and voluntariness.** The treating physiotherapist or a clinic colleague who is not the researcher invites patients; patients are told clearly that declining the camera feature has **no effect on their care**, that the exercises remain exactly as assigned, and that they can stop at any time. The researcher does not recruit patients of whom they are the sole treating therapist if the committee advises against it [TO COMPLETE].

## 5. Procedures

1. **Information and consent** (separate patient information sheet and consent form, document 06), in English or Urdu, with time to decide; the patient is told that the camera is optional for every session.
2. **Eligibility check** by the treating physiotherapist, recorded on the screening form (document 10). The researcher checks it.
3. **Baseline:** age band, sex, region, the assigned exercises, pain (NPRS), and the outcome measures the clinic already uses; recorded in the application as part of care.
4. **Session 1, in clinic:** the physiotherapist sets up the phone with the patient, explains the on-screen picture and the stop rule, and watches the first sets. For a sample of sets the physiotherapist counts and rates the movement independently of the tool (the tool's cue is not visible to the physiotherapist's rating until it is entered).
5. **Following sessions** ([TO COMPLETE: choose]): (a) all sessions in clinic; or (b) home sessions with the camera at the patient's choice, the physiotherapist reviewing the numeric summary (never a video) at each visit, a contact line for questions, and the same stop rule.
6. **Closing visit:** SUS, comfort and acceptability items, a short interview, adverse-event review.

## 6. The tool in the pilot

The tool is used under the constraints of Part A: it shows a repetition count and hold timer; it shows a numeric angle **only** for movement types that met the pre-specified rules; it gives only fixed cues that never ask for more range, effort or force and never ride over pain; it states what it cannot see (for example spinal curvature, pelvic tilt, whether deep abdominal muscles are working); and it always shows "Stop if anything hurts, feels dizzy or tingles." The tool does not diagnose, does not alter the exercise prescription and does not judge whether it is safe to exercise. The physiotherapist can switch the camera feature off for any patient at any time.

## 7. Safety, stopping rules and oversight

- A physiotherapist is present for the first session and available afterwards [TO COMPLETE: how and when].
- Any new or increased pain, dizziness, tingling or other symptom stops the movement; the patient is asked about it before the next one; the physiotherapist decides whether to continue, change the exercise or refer.
- A patient who has an exercise-related pain increase of [TO COMPLETE: for example 2 or more NPRS points that lasts more than 24 hours] attributed to the use of the tool leaves the camera part of the study; the event is recorded and reviewed.
- **Study-level stop:** if two or more serious events occur, or one serious event judged related to the tool, recruitment stops and the committee is informed; the principal investigator and an independent clinician review before any restart.
- Adverse events are recorded on the form (document 10) and reported as the committee requires.

## 8. Data

Per set: the patient code, the exercise, counts, hold times, the angle only for validated movement types and its confidence, whether the tool refused and why, cue events (type and time), the physiotherapist's own count and rating where observed, and condition categories (phone, lighting, clothing category). Per patient: baseline and closing measures, SUS, comfort items, adverse events. **No video, no image and no landmark coordinates are stored.** The patient's name is not stored with the study data; the key is held by the principal investigator separately. Patients' routine clinical records remain under the clinic's own record rules and are not altered by the study. Retention [TO COMPLETE]. See the data management plan (document 07).

## 9. Statistical analysis (descriptive)

No hypothesis tests. Proportions with exact 95% confidence intervals for completion, coverage, adverse events and agreement of counts and cues; Bland–Altman style differences for counts and hold times; Cohen's kappa for cue versus physiotherapist rating; SUS scores with the usual interpretation bands; qualitative comments summarised thematically by two readers. Missing data are reported, not imputed. The independent analyst runs the analysis on the de-identified export. With 8 to 10 patients the intervals will be wide and are reported as such; the pilot's purpose is to learn whether and how to proceed, not to estimate effects.

## 10. Ethical considerations

**Risk.** Small: the exercises are those the patient is already assigned, in the dose assigned; the camera adds no load. The main risks are a misleading cue (controlled by the fixed wording rules and the stop rule), distraction from the exercise (controlled by a simple view and by the physiotherapist) and embarrassment about the camera (nothing is recorded; the camera is optional).

**Benefit.** Possible, not promised: some patients may find the feedback useful. No benefit is claimed.

**Consent and voluntariness.** Written, with a clear statement that care is unaffected by declining or withdrawing; the information sheet is available in English and Roman Urdu (Urdu-script version if the committee requires it).

**Privacy.** On-device processing; no recording; code instead of name; the physiotherapist sees a numeric summary only.

**Conflict of interest.** As in Part A; the analysis plan is fixed before the first patient, the analyst is independent, and all results including failures are reported.

**Regulatory position.** The software is used for exercise guidance and research in this pilot; the position under the medical device rules that apply in [TO COMPLETE: jurisdiction] will be confirmed before the pilot and before any wider release.
